Page-specific full-text findings (one-based physical PDF pages):

- **Temporal information:** pp. 2–4 derive time-feasible CGR routes before optimization. The MILP selects `y_p`, `z_k`, and `x_sd`; these variables have route/scope/pair indices, not a time index. Thus temporal routing feasibility is present, while the displayed provisioning optimization does not schedule operation start times.
- **Lifetime, capacity, and SAs:** pp. 4–5 constrain connectivity, pair/route consistency, and required scope availability. These equations do not contain key-age/cryptoperiod, expiry/rotation, per-contact installation capacity, or endpoint SA state-transition variables. Page 2 does discuss long-span security associations; do not claim SAs are absent from the work's concepts.
- **Experiments and horizon:** p. 5 fixes a 2700 s contact-plan horizon, evaluates up to three routes per ordered pair, and sweeps target connectivity in 5% increments. Its data-at-rest exposure is a route/intermediate-node metric, not a traffic-key disclosure/recovery experiment. The reported evaluation does not vary a controller's visible contact horizon. Page 6 explicitly identifies key scheduling over contact plans as future work; slide 7 agrees.
- **Scope of inference:** these are distinctions from the six-page model and reported evaluation, not a claim about an inspected repository implementation or all prior DTN security work. The cited DTKA/BERMUDA works were not independently reviewed in this follow-up. No external system was reimplemented or experimentally outperformed by R1.

## Bounded wording for the article

> Contact-aware routing, advance authentication, and connectivity-dependent authorization updates are established. In particular, Detke et al. couple time-feasible CGR routes with offline BPSec key-scope provisioning, and identify key scheduling over contact plans as future work. Our study formulates and evaluates sequential traffic-key and SA operations for a fixed spacecraft-ground workload, with a shared executor, constraints, costs, and objective. Within each fixed full-plan tie rule, the controlled comparison changes only the permitted contact horizon. This distinguishes the evaluated question; it does not establish priority over all temporal key-management formulations.

This proposed distinction is an inference from the accessed sources and the implemented scope. For Detke it is now supported by the retrieved formulation and future-work statement, with the model/evaluation boundaries above. No cited external system was reimplemented or experimentally outperformed by R1.
